# Study craft worksheets

_Two school-format worksheets that turned out to be the most directly useful
non-book items in the folder, because this course's failure mode is behavioural,
not technical._

## `Focused-Silence-A-Path-to-Deep-Work-4.pdf`

Two pages, ~360 words, in worksheet form (Name / Date / Section / Score). It defines
focused silence as a deliberate and structured period of quiet work and states the
rules plainly. It is the written contract for the 40-60 minute block each day assumes
you will actually protect.

Use it the way the course intends:

1. Before opening a day, fill in the header by hand - name, date, section, and what
   you expect your score to be.
2. Read only section 1 (Recall first) before the silence starts. The recall question
   is the door; if you skip it you arrive at the author's text already half-reading.
3. At the end, write the score yourself and leave the sheet in the folder. A gate you
   graded on paper is harder to talk yourself through than a green tick on a screen.

## `Simple-Writing-Prompt-Worksheet-17.pdf`

A "Mad Lib Story" prompt sheet: it collects answers to a fixed list of questions and
reassembles them into a story. That is a child's worksheet, and it is also the exact
structure of Days 38 and 39 - task, audience, format supplied separately, then
composed. If you have ever written a 200-word prompt that still produced a useless
answer, this worksheet is the diagnosis.

## Why these two are on the shelf and not the spine

They are short and they are about you rather than the subject. The spine carries the
author's words; the shelf carries what you do with them. When a day's gate keeps
failing, come here before you come to the next day.
